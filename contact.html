The Story of Geneva's Trading Post

Handcrafted rabbit fur hat from our own hand-tanned pelts

I grew up between Sitka, Alaska and the San Juan Islands. 4‑H was a constant through my childhood—nine years as a member, then five more as an adult leader before I left for college. That program taught me everything from textile arts to astronomy, and it's come full circle now: I'm currently becoming a leader for Gustavus Country Critters.

By high school, I was already running a sole proprietorship, selling my art and animals at craft fairs. I started with meat rabbits at seventeen. From there, I went to Evergreen for dual degrees in physics and math, with my math degree focused on nonlinear modeling of biological complex systems—a thread that would carry forward.

I came to Fairbanks to start a physics Ph.D. studying auroras. Along the way, I shifted gears and earned an MS in Physics with an emphasis in biological modeling and nonlinear dynamics—picking up right where my Evergreen work left off. During grad school, I bought my house in Fairbanks, and homesteading became my anchor. Through every twist and turn, the animals and the garden kept me grounded.

I married my husband in 2019. When COVID hit the next year, my off‑grid skills weren't just hobbies anymore—they carried me through seven layoffs in twelve months. In 2021, instead of pulling back, we doubled down: we bought our off‑grid Gustavus homestead near Glacier Bay while keeping our original Fairbanks home.

Today, I'm a Certified Master Gardener (2024) and an active ARBA member. I'm back at UAF for more training, and I still find time to be an amateur astronomer. The details have changed over the years, but the core of what I do hasn't: raising animals, growing food, and building something that lasts.
