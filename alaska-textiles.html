All Natural Fiber Glacier Bay National Park

Flora of Glacier Bay

A collection celebrating the edible and iconic plants of Southeast Alaska. Each potholder captures the unique colors and shapes of our local mushrooms and berries.

Fly Amanita: Despite its striking appearance, this mushroom is toxic. It forms symbiotic relationships with spruce and birch trees.

Salmonberry: Named for its salmon-colored berries that appear when the salmon run. Both berries and young shoots are edible.

Winter Chanterelle: One of Alaska's most prized edible mushrooms, found from late summer through winter in coastal rainforests.

🐋 Orca (Killer Whale) – Apex predators of Alaska's waters, orcas hunt in family pods and communicate with complex vocalizations.
🐙 Giant Pacific Octopus – The largest octopus species, with an arm span up to 20 feet. They have three hearts and blue blood!
🐋 Humpback Whale – Known for their haunting songs and acrobatic breaches. They travel 3,000+ miles to feed in Alaska's waters.
🦦 Sea Otter – The smallest marine mammal, with the densest fur of any animal. They hold hands while sleeping to stay together!
All Natural Fiber Pacific Northwest Waters

Sea Life of Alaska

A celebration of the incredible marine creatures that inhabit Alaska's coastal waters. Each piece captures the unique characteristics of these beloved animals.

Orca: Resident orcas eat fish; transient orcas hunt marine mammals. Each pod has its own unique dialect.

Octopus: Highly intelligent, with problem-solving abilities and camouflage skills. They can squeeze through any opening larger than their beak.

Humpback Whale: Males sing complex songs that can travel for miles underwater. Each population has its own unique song that evolves over time.

Sea Otter: Crucial to kelp forest ecosystems. They eat 25% of their body weight daily to stay warm.

🐿️ Hoary Marmot – These alpine dwellers whistle to warn of danger. They hibernate 7-8 months each year!
🐻 Brown Bear (Grizzly) – Alaska's iconic bears. A coastal brown bear can eat 80-90 salmon per day during the salmon run!

Land Mammals of Alaska

Honoring the creatures that roam Alaska's diverse landscapes, from alpine meadows to coastal forests.

Hoary Marmot: Found in Alaska's mountain ranges, these large ground squirrels live in colonies and use distinct alarm calls for different predators.

Brown Bear: Alaska is home to an estimated 30,000 brown bears. They can run 35 mph and have an incredible sense of smell—far better than a bloodhound's.
